AI stock analysis prompts for ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok

Published November 6, 2026 · Last updated November 6, 2026 · By the QuantCall AI team

These 7 stock analysis prompts work in any major chatbot. Replace [TICKER] with the stock you're researching. Start with the data-freshness prompt, then force an equal bull and bear case. Those two catch the most common problems: stale information and one-sided answers.

The 7 prompts

1. Check how fresh its data is

What is the date of the newest information you're using on [TICKER]? List your sources.

Do this first. If the answer is months old, so is everything after it.

2. Force both sides

Give me the bull case and the bear case for [TICKER], equal length, with the evidence for each.

Stops the answer from drifting into one-sided hype.

3. Check the latest earnings

What did [TICKER] report in its last earnings release? Compare revenue, EPS and guidance with expectations, and say how the stock reacted.

A quick test of whether it actually knows recent news. Verify against the company's release.

4. Find the hidden risks

What would have to be true for [TICKER] to fall 30%? Rank those scenarios by how plausible they are.

Surfaces risks a normal summary glosses over.

5. Test its confidence

Which of your points about [TICKER] are you least sure about, and what would change your mind?

A useful answer admits uncertainty.

6. Compare to peers

Compare [TICKER] with its 3 closest competitors on growth, margins and valuation in a table. Note the date of each number.

Valuation only means something next to peers. Check the numbers.

7. Explain a big move

Why did [TICKER] move today? Separate confirmed facts from speculation, and cite sources.

Good for red days, when explanations get overconfident.

Which chatbot to use them in

  • Any model with live web access is better for recent news and earnings. Without it, answers may be out of date, which is why prompt 1 comes first.

Common questions

What is the best prompt for stock analysis with AI?

Start by asking how recent the data is, then ask for an equal-length bull and bear case. Those two prompts catch the most common problems: stale information and one-sided answers.

Can I use ChatGPT to analyze a stock?

You can use it for research, such as summarizing earnings or listing risks. Check the facts against primary sources, and don't treat the output as advice.

Do these prompts work in Claude, Gemini, Perplexity and Grok?

Yes. They're plain-language prompts. Results vary by model and by whether it has live web access.
